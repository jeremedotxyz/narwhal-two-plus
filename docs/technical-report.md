# Narwhal Two Plus: Design Basis and Reference Software for a Concept Underwater Scanning LiDAR

**Technical design report, version 0.1.0 · 21 October 2026**  
**Project:** Narwhal Two Plus  
**Drawing assembly:** NEREID-532, revision A  
**Evidence classification:** analytical design study and synthetic reference implementation  
**Publication status:** project technical report; not peer reviewed

## Abstract

This report defines an underwater scanning light detection and ranging (LiDAR) concept using a pulsed 532 nm source, a single-axis transmit scanner, a separate wide-field receiver and a dry pressure housing. The proposed instrument targets short-range subsea inspection. Its initial objectives are a 30 degree in-water fan, a 1-15 m operating range in clear water, a 20 mm single-return precision at 5 m under specified laboratory conditions, and a 300 m deployment depth. These objectives have not been demonstrated. The contribution is an explicit engineering design basis, three vector drawing sheets, a separate sensor-data sheet, a proposed data contract and executable software that generates synthetic returns, reconstructs bistatic ranges, replays input delays and exports point clouds. First-order calculations yield approximately 44.70 ns round-trip delay at 5 m, 55.93 mm monostatic range spacing for 0.5 ns sampling, and 3.02 MPa hydrostatic differential pressure at 300 m under the stated assumptions. Numerical tests verify geometry and software behavior, not optical detectability, pressure integrity or hardware accuracy. Missing component designs and validation work are stated as release gates.

**Keywords:** underwater LiDAR; direct time of flight; bistatic geometry; subsea metrology; reference implementation; pressure housing.

## 1. Scope and scientific status

The project is intended to make a proposed sensor inspectable and reproducible before hardware procurement. It is not an experimentally validated instrument description. Four evidence classes are maintained throughout:

1. **Design assumptions:** selected wavelengths, envelope dimensions, budgets and goals.
2. **Derived quantities:** values computed from specified formulas and inputs.
3. **Synthetic results:** outputs generated by the reference simulator.
4. **Measured results:** currently none.

The drawings establish an assembly arrangement and functional interfaces. They do not contain a complete optical prescription, electrical netlist, PCB layout, pressure qualification package or manufacturing tolerances. No STEP model or native solid-model CAD is supplied. The editable source produces vector PDF drawings; the source is not a parametric 3D mechanical model.

The requested software has therefore been implemented as an executable research reference, with a clearly bounded interface for later hardware integration. A driver cannot be completed against unspecified ADC registers, FPGA logic, a galvo amplifier or a laser controller. Pretending those devices exist would make the package less useful for real engineering.

## 2. Background and architectural choice

Published underwater LiDAR research examines non-coaxial transmitter/receiver arrangements and mitigation of near-field water backscatter [1]. This supplies background for considering separate optical channels; it does not validate the geometry or performance proposed here. The NEREID-532 architecture uses one receive channel, whereas [1] considers a multi-channel system. No claim of reproducing that instrument or its experiments is made.

A green pulsed source, a scanning transmitter and an APD-based direct receiver are adopted as design assumptions. Component selection must determine pulse stability, divergence, receiver bandwidth, noise, dynamic range and attainable timing estimation. A 532 nm, 1 nm receiver filter is an initial target whose spectral behavior across the proposed wide field requires ray tracing. Filter passband shifts and receiver etendue may invalidate the initial arrangement.

Separate windows permit independent beam conditioning and receive optics. They introduce a 60 mm vertical baseline, field overlap and range-dependent parallax. A one-axis scan simplifies the mechanism; a second mapping dimension requires calibrated vehicle motion or a later second-axis mechanism. The current software does not infer vehicle poses or produce georeferenced surveys.

## 3. System definition

### 3.1 Mechanical arrangement

The nominal cylindrical tube is 140 mm outside diameter and 120 mm inside diameter. Front and rear flanges have a maximum diameter of 160 mm. Body length is 320 mm; the connector envelope adds 39 mm. Mount saddles use four nominal 8.5 mm through holes on a 220 by 90 mm rectangular pattern. The saddles avoid through-wall mounting holes.

Transmit and receive clear apertures are 32 mm and 60 mm respectively, centered 60 mm apart vertically. Window material is provisionally sapphire. Blank diameter, thickness, seat geometry, retainer threads, bolt preload, seal compound and gland dimensions remain unresolved. The section drawing places component envelopes inside the housing; optical spacing and retention details are schematic.

The proposed tube and flange material is titanium Grade 5. This is a concept material selection rather than an approved material specification. Corrosion, coating compatibility, galvanic isolation, trapped water, venting and assembly serviceability require review. Proposed dual radial body seals and inter-seal leakage monitoring are design provisions, not a leak-tightness guarantee.

### 3.2 Optical and electrical chain

The outgoing path is driver → pulsed source → collimator and galvo → transmit window → water and target. A monitor photodiode should establish an emission-time reference for each pulse. The receiver path is window and lens → optical filter → APD → transimpedance amplifier and limiter → digitizer → FPGA timing → CPU and Ethernet output.

Nominal source assumptions are 5 microjoules, 2 ns pulse width and 20,000 pulses per second. Average optical power before losses is 0.10 W. The 35 W nominal and 60 W peak electrical budgets are allocations; they do not follow from a measured component power budget.

Input power is 18-30 VDC. Protection, isolation, filtering, analog/digital supplies and chassis bonding need a component-level design. An independent hardware permit and fault chain is proposed. The Python software has no emission control path, and software tests cannot verify a hardware safety function.

### 3.3 Timing and output

At 20 kHz, pulse spacing is 50 microseconds. One thousand angular samples produce at most 20 lines/s before flyback, rejected returns or averaging. A 2 GS/s digitizer has 0.5 ns sample intervals. The architecture therefore requires a sub-sample timing estimator to approach the 20 mm precision objective. The reference software consumes already estimated time delays; it does not implement waveform acquisition or peak estimation.

Outputs use a sensor frame with +Z forward, +X starboard and +Y down. The origin is the midpoint of the optical centers at the front reference plane. Proposed synchronized timestamps use TAI nanoseconds from 1970-01-01 00:00:00 TAI; the simulator uses a zero-origin relative clock and sets an unsynchronized flag. A calibration ID and metadata sidecar accompany each output dataset.

## 4. Measurement model

### 4.1 Travel time and water indices

Let c0 = 299,792,458 m/s, the exact vacuum light speed [2]. With group refractive index ng, a calibrated delay dt corresponds to total water path L:

```math
L = c_0 (t_{observed} - t_{system}) / n_g.
```

The reference uses ng = 1.34 as an assumption. Phase index np = 1.333 is used only for illustrative refraction. The supplied temperature and salinity values are recorded metadata, not inputs to a refractive-index equation. Port transit, electronics delays and internal optical paths must be removed or incorporated in a calibrated model before physical data can be interpreted this way.

For a monostatic or sufficiently distant target, R ≈ L/2. At 5 m the monostatic-equivalent time is approximately 44.70 ns. A 20 mm range increment corresponds to approximately 179 ps. Neither figure proves a particular estimator can deliver that precision.

### 4.2 Exact bistatic intersection

Let T and Q be transmitter and receiver origins, b = Q − T, u a calibrated unit transmit ray, and P = T + r u a point along that ray. For homogeneous water:

```math
L = r + ||r u - b||,
\qquad r = \frac{L^2 - ||b||^2}{2(L-u\cdot b)}.
```

The code rejects delays shorter than the direct baseline path. The default origins are T = (0, −0.03, 0) m and Q = (0, +0.03, 0) m, and u = (sin a, 0, cos a). Geometry tests also use oblique rays with nonzero vertical components to check the general expression against independently constructed point distances.

This model assumes the calibrated ray has already emerged from the port. The effective origins and delays in a true flat-port system require geometric calibration. The expression does not solve scattering, multipath or refractive-index gradients.

### 4.3 Scan, refraction and sampling

For ideal parallel port faces, the illustrative net Snell relation is sin(a_air) = np sin(a_water). Sapphire thickness changes ray displacement and transit time even where the net angular relation is simple. The software provides an angle conversion helper; simulated event angles are already water-side angles and are not refracted again during reconstruction.

For N samples over a field F, the simulator uses pixel centers a_i = F((i+0.5)/N − 0.5). A 30 degree field with 1,000 samples has a 0.03 degree nominal increment; the first and last sample centers lie slightly inside the field edges. The approximate boresight pitch at 5 m is 2.62 mm. A 2 mrad divergence contributes roughly 10 mm of beam diameter growth over 5 m. Angular sample spacing therefore is not equivalent to resolving 2.62 mm features.

### 4.4 Attenuation and pressure calculations

The illustrative two-way homogeneous transmission factor is exp(−c_att L), reducing to exp(−2 c_att R) in the monostatic limit. At 10 m, c_att = 0.10/m gives about 0.135 and c_att = 0.30/m gives about 0.00248. These omit target reflectance, collection geometry, scattering into the field, noise and saturation. The simulator's intensity counts are arbitrary scaled values, not a radiometric link budget.

For density 1,025 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s² and 300 m depth, p = rho g h gives 3.016575 MPa gauge differential pressure relative to a dry housing near surface pressure. Multiplying by the area of a 60 mm clear aperture yields approximately 8.53 kN. Actual seat loads depend on unsupported geometry. Tube collapse, end-cap bending, windows, bolts, fatigue and seals remain unanalyzed.

## 5. Reproducible numerical methods

The reference experiment uses the versioned concept JSON, seed 532, a plane at Z = 5 m, two lines of 1,000 shots, 0.1 ns Gaussian timing jitter and an independent 0.02 dropout probability. True points are found by intersecting each transmit ray with the plane. The bistatic path is computed from those points; Gaussian jitter is added to delay; reconstruction then uses the inverse model.

The assumed timing jitter is chosen to exercise the software, not derived from laser pulse width, APD noise or an actual ADC. Dropout is an arbitrary Bernoulli process independent of computed intensity. The model does not generate water-backscatter waveforms, multiple returns, digitizer quantization, scan dynamics, optical blur, correlated jitter, target reflectance distributions or ambient illumination.

Run the [experiment script](../examples/reproduce.py) from the repository root:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python3 examples/reproduce.py
```

It writes a machine-readable result and Markdown results table under `runs/experiment/`. The committed [numerical result](../examples/reference-results.md) is a snapshot produced by that script. A small [synthetic raw sample](../examples/synthetic-sample.raw.jsonl) demonstrates the input schema.

No-noise geometric recovery is a correctness check using the same physical assumptions in both directions. It establishes numerical consistency; it is not independent validation of a sensor's measurement physics. Separate tests construct arbitrary target points to verify the closed-form geometry against path lengths.

## 6. Results and interpretation

First-order results are generated by `narwhal metrics`. See the [committed experiment results](../examples/reference-results.md) for exact software output and sample counts. Unit/integration checks cover general bistatic intersection, monostatic reduction, Snell consistency, invalid configuration, nonphysical delays, dropout, range gating, reproducibility, binary layout, CRC, reserved fields, point validity, replay identity and actual localhost UDP reception.

An assumed timing standard deviation of 0.1 ns implies approximately 11.19 mm monostatic range standard deviation before additional error sources. A synthetic plane result near that number demonstrates the chosen noise setting. It is not evidence that an APD/ADC chain meets a 20 mm target. Validity flags and the metadata preserve this distinction.

The receiver test uses packets generated by the reference codec. It establishes that the host CLI can consume that proposed format over a loopback socket. It does not establish compatibility with an ADC board, FPGA or subsea Ethernet interface.

## 7. Uncertainty and validation requirements

A physical uncertainty budget must include source timing, pulse shape, estimator bias, APD gain/noise, ADC aperture jitter, analog saturation, clock calibration, water group index, port geometry, ray direction, baseline, thermal drift and pose alignment. Sample pitch, precision, accuracy and spatial resolution must be reported independently. For small errors in the monostatic approximation, timing contributes c0 sigma_t/(2 ng), while group-index error contributes approximately R sigma_ng/ng. Bistatic sensitivities should be evaluated at each calibrated ray.

Range testing should use at least 1,000 valid returns per target position at 1, 5, 10 and 15 m, while also reporting all transmitted shots and dropout rates. Record target reflectance, attenuation measurement, ambient light, water temperature/salinity, geometry, reference distance and traceability. Report bias and one-sigma dispersion separately. Any precision pass threshold requires frozen test conditions.

Pressure qualification requires reviewed external-pressure calculations and an approved proof/fatigue protocol; this report does not prescribe a test pressure. Thermal testing should cover still-water heat rejection and peak duty cycle. Electrical validation must verify faults, isolation, EMC, leakage and corrosion. Laser classification and accessible-emission assessment must cover assembly, maintenance, optical faults and dry operation.

## 8. Limitations, availability and next steps

The deliverables are available in this repository and can be reproduced locally with Python. Drawings require ReportLab, and extracting the standalone data PDF additionally requires pypdf. Core simulation and data tools have no third-party runtime dependency. No measured dataset, performance certification, manufactured assembly or procurement-ready bill of materials is available.

The next engineering gates are an optical feasibility study and link budget; selection of source/galvo/APD/ADC/FPGA; pressure and window design; clock and calibration implementation; bench validation; tank measurements; and subsea trials. Detailed gates are listed in the [roadmap](roadmap.md). Changes to these assumptions must propagate to both documentation and code.
